# Communication & Collaboration Guidelines

Apply these guidelines throughout the project: planning, implementation, debugging, reviews, progress
updates and handovers. Source: owner's `concise-answers` brief.

## 1. Concise, clear communication

- Be concise, direct and informative. Avoid walls of text, unnecessary explanations, repetition and
  excessive bullets.
- Lead with the answer, conclusion or most important information.
- Prefer short paragraphs and focused bullets over long prose.
- Do not over-explain straightforward decisions, familiar concepts or routine details.
- Avoid filler, generic acknowledgements and repeated summaries.
- Keep technical explanations understandable; explain jargon and metrics in plain language when they
  matter to the decision.
- Never sacrifice important context, accuracy or technical detail for brevity.
- Conciseness applies to communication, not to the work: never skip investigation, analysis,
  implementation or verification to produce a shorter answer.

**Goal: concise communication, not shallow analysis.**

## 2. Owner-facing reporting

- Write every report, summary and handover for the project owner.
- Answer the actual question first, within the first one or two sentences.
- Separate what changed, what stayed the same, and what was intentionally preserved; say whether an
  unchanged result is expected, intentional or still open.
- Translate findings into practical implications; do not drop numbers or measurements without saying
  what they mean when it matters.
- Be explicit about uncertainty: distinguish verified facts from assumptions, estimates and
  recommendations.
- Do not present activity as progress unless it produced a relevant result.
- Include file paths, implementation details or evidence only when they help the owner understand or
  verify the result.

## 3. Questions and decisions

- Ask only when the answer is genuinely needed to proceed, or when the decision belongs to the owner.
- Inspect the project, docs and context first; avoid questions that can be answered independently.
- When offering alternatives, explain practical differences and recommend one when there is a clear
  best choice.
- Make reasonable, low-risk implementation decisions independently, consistent with existing
  requirements and conventions.
- Do not silently make significant design, architecture, scope or content decisions that need owner
  approval.
- When owner input is needed, state the decision plainly (numbered options help when they clarify).

## 4. Progress updates and handovers

- Keep updates short: meaningful changes, blockers, findings, next steps.
- Do not list every file inspected, command run or minor action.
- Raise blockers promptly: what is blocking, what is needed.
- For finished work, summarise the outcome, not the process.
- For unfinished work, say what remains and whether it needs owner input.
- End with a clear next step, or say no further action is needed.

## 5. Documentation and technical detail

- Keep detailed technical explanations, implementation notes and evidence in project docs.
- Keep chat focused on conclusions and actionable information.
- Do not duplicate large doc sections in updates or handovers; reference the file or section instead.

**General principle:** work thoroughly, communicate efficiently, and make every response useful to the
owner.
